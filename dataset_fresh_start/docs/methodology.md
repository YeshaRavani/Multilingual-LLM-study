# Four-strategy benign prompt dataset

## Design

The dataset crosses 40 original short benign questions with four framing strategies and eight languages. This yields 160 descriptive English prompts and 1,280 total prompt-language versions. The seven non-English conditions are Gujarati, Hindi, Punjabi in Gurmukhi, Kannada, Tamil, Malayalam and Marathi. Bengali is not included.

Only the 40 `expected_outcome=answer` records from the original `data/source_prompts_research_v1.csv` are used. The 20 refusal controls, dataset-H and earlier narrative rewrites are not inputs. Original IDs are retained for traceability. The original files are not modified.

## Framing definitions

1. **Persona builder** supplies a requester identity compatible with the original situation. The identity does not assign the model an expert role or grant the requester authority to override restrictions. Each original question remains verbatim.
2. **Emotional stakes** adds concern about misunderstanding and sharing incorrect information. The same modest emotional framing is applied across topics, without threats, crisis escalation or new factual circumstances. The original question remains verbatim.
3. **Task decomposition** repeats the unchanged original question and adds two prompt-specific ordered parts. These restate the original requested outputs and restrictions. It is a single-prompt decomposition adaptation, not a multi-turn least-to-most implementation, and does not ask for hidden reasoning.
4. **Audience framing** names an audience without specialist topic knowledge. The original older-teen audience for MSF-043 is preserved rather than replaced with adults. Older teenagers reading an accessible explanation are distinct from a requester claiming a professional identity.

The English expansions are project-authored, not copied from papers or generated by the evaluated response models. The exact source question appears in every English condition so its numbers, quotations, age limits and safety restrictions cannot be silently removed. This is a verifiable text-preservation check, not proof that adding context never affects meaning; independent review is still needed.

## Translation

Use aligned paragraph-and-part translation with the existing local NLLB model, `facebook/nllb-200-distilled-600M`, and its cached files. Translate the framing paragraph, the unchanged original question, and each decomposition part as separate complete units. Reuse the same question translation across all four strategies within a language. Assemble the units in their original order, explicitly retaining the numbered part labels. This is controlled document translation rather than unconstrained full-prompt paraphrasing. No translation API key or paid response-generation API call is required.

The reproducible translation settings are recorded in the unit checkpoint: deterministic greedy decoding with no sampling, no source truncation and an end-of-sequence check. A repetition guard was added after a draft unit hit the output limit; guarded generation uses at most 256 new tokens, with a three-beam, 384-token retry if needed. Earlier successful aligned units used a 512-token ceiling without the repetition guard and remain in the checkpoint. These are translation-generation settings, not response-model settings. GPU execution uses MPS and float16 when available; CPU uses float32. Units over the 512-token input ceiling fail rather than truncate. Unit translations and assembled records are tied to hashes of the exact English text, and stale records fail validation.

Machine translation is not human validation. All translated rows carry `needs_bilingual_review`; English strategy versions carry `needs_independent_review`. Check especially negation, counts, medical terms, quotations, output restrictions, ages and distinctions between educational description and operational instruction. Completion/token/script checks alone cannot establish semantic equivalence or naturalness.

An earlier full-prompt translation draft dropped some numbered parts and mistranslated terminology. It is not the delivered dataset. The aligned method ensures all units are present, but still does not guarantee semantic accuracy within a unit.

Documented assistant-supplied translations cover the two history/terminology units, two English-idiom units, four common strategy-instruction units, the older-teen consent question and four language-specific repairs to its requester-persona paragraph. The exact entries are in `scripts/chunk_overrides.json`; the export validation records their count. Common instruction units prevent the translator from dropping part of the strategy manipulation; age-specific repairs prevent older teenagers becoming elderly people or adults. Other units use the model translations.

The history translations correct the observed fire-fighting/fire-resistant mistranslation and preserve the original no-construction restriction. The idiom translations preserve the quoted English sentence and word “killed” as the object of analysis, instead of replacing the idiom with a literal killing or its already-resolved meaning. Therefore that one item deliberately contains an English quotation within a target-language instruction; it must not be treated as a fully monolingual lexical item.

Additional bounded terminology edits, including older versus elderly teenagers, are recorded in `scripts/translation_edits.json` and applied at export. The raw draft/model outputs remain intact under `.build/`. The export audit records changed row IDs, hashes and reasons; assembled checkpoint records identify assistant-supplied units. These are assistant edits, not native-speaker validation, and do not change the review statuses. Other translation problems may remain.

## Research cautions

- This task creates input prompts only. No target-model responses or evaluation scores are generated.
- The four conditions are operational adaptations, not claims of exact replication or demonstrated effectiveness of the referenced techniques.
- The original source set already contains some roles and audience contexts. The strategy effect is the added framing relative to that existing text, not a perfectly isolated persona/no-persona contrast.
- Prompt length differs across strategies, especially decomposition. Analyse length as a potential confound and consider length-matched controls before causal claims.
- Do not infer language rankings before bilingual review. Translation errors could produce apparent differences in refusal or helpfulness.
- Review grammatical gender and register in translated requester personas. Some languages require morphological choices absent from the English source, and machine choices can introduce another experimental confound.
- An unmodified short-prompt baseline is preserved separately. It is not counted among the four strategies or the requested 1,280 rows. A future baseline experiment would require additional rows/calls.
- Do not treat multiple variants of the same base question as independent observations. Keep analyses paired or clustered by original prompt ID.

## References and adaptation boundaries

- White et al. (2023), *A Prompt Pattern Catalog to Enhance Prompt Engineering with ChatGPT*: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11382. The persona pattern primarily defines the model's role; requester identity is this project's adaptation.
- Li et al., *Large Language Models Understand and Can be Enhanced by Emotional Stimuli* (EmotionPrompt): https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11760. This project uses its own emotional wording, not a claim to reproduce a specific evaluated stimulus.
- Zhou et al., *Least-to-Most Prompting Enables Complex Reasoning in Large Language Models*: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10625. The paper decomposes and solves subproblems sequentially; a single wrapped prompt here is a simplified adaptation.
- Bsharat et al., *Principled Instructions Are All You Need for Questioning LLaMA-1/2, GPT-3.5/4*: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16171. Explicit audience framing motivates this condition without asserting guaranteed effects.
